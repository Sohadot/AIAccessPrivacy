# AI Access Privacy

## Category Thesis

Artificial intelligence has entered a new operational era.

Modern AI systems no longer generate text in isolation.
They retrieve documents, access databases, invoke tools, execute workflows, communicate with external services, and increasingly operate through autonomous or semi-autonomous agents.

This evolution changes the primary governance question.

The central question is no longer:

> "What can an AI system do?"

The central question becomes:

> "Under what conditions should an AI system be allowed to access data, identities, tools, services, and execution capabilities?"

This question does not belong entirely to information security.

It does not belong entirely to privacy.

It does not belong entirely to identity management.

It does not belong entirely to AI governance.

Instead, it exists at the intersection of all of them.

Yet today this intersection has no independent reference category.

Existing frameworks explain authentication.

Authorization frameworks describe delegated permissions.

Identity frameworks establish who is requesting access.

Privacy regulations define legal obligations.

Security frameworks reduce technical risk.

AI governance frameworks define organizational responsibilities.

However, none of these establish AI access itself as a first-class governance object.

AI Access Privacy defines this missing category.

---

## Definition

AI Access Privacy is the discipline of defining, declaring, verifying, governing, and continuously observing the conditions under which artificial intelligence systems may access data, identities, tools, services, and execution capabilities.

The object of governance is not the AI model itself.

The object of governance is the relationship between an AI system and the resources it is permitted to access.

---

## Why This Category Exists

As AI systems evolve from assistants into operational actors, access becomes more consequential than intelligence.

An advanced model without access cannot materially affect systems.

A modest model with unrestricted access can.

Risk therefore emerges not only from model capability, but from access capability.

This creates a governance gap that existing disciplines only partially address.

AI Access Privacy exists to close that gap.

---

## Scope

This category concerns:

- access boundaries
- declared permissions
- execution capabilities
- data exposure
- identity relationships
- authorization context
- verification status
- evidence of declared behavior
- continuous observation of access posture
- revocation and lifecycle management

This category does not attempt to replace:

- authentication
- authorization
- identity standards
- privacy regulation
- security frameworks
- AI model evaluation

Instead, it provides the reference layer through which these domains become observable as part of AI access.

---

## Mission

The mission of AI Access Privacy is to establish a vendor-neutral reference framework through which AI access can be described, understood, evaluated, verified, monitored, and governed consistently across systems.

---

## Long-Term Objective

To establish AI Access Privacy as the canonical reference category for governed AI access, enabling interoperable standards, machine-readable declarations, operational tooling, and trusted governance across the AI ecosystem.
