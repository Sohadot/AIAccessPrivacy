# AI Access Privacy

## Category Thesis

Artificial intelligence systems are no longer isolated software tools.

They browse the web, read documents, access databases, connect to APIs,
operate enterprise systems, invoke external tools, and increasingly act
through autonomous or semi-autonomous agents.

As AI capabilities expand, the fundamental question is no longer:

> "Can an AI perform a task?"

The fundamental question becomes:

> "Under what conditions should an AI be allowed to access data, tools,
> identities, and execution capabilities?"

Today there is no universally accepted reference layer describing,
classifying, declaring, verifying, and governing AI access.

Security standards describe authentication.

Identity standards describe who is requesting access.

Privacy regulations describe legal obligations.

Authorization frameworks describe delegated permissions.

Yet none of them define AI access itself as a first-class governance object.

AI Access Privacy introduces this missing category.

It defines AI access as a governable object whose permissions,
boundaries, evidence, verification status, and revocation state
must be explicit, machine-readable, independently verifiable,
and continuously observable.

This project does not compete with AI providers.

It does not replace authorization protocols.

It does not replace privacy regulation.

Instead, it provides the reference layer that connects them.

Its mission is to establish a canonical framework through which
humans, organizations, software agents, and AI systems can describe,
evaluate, verify, and monitor AI access in a consistent and
vendor-neutral manner.

The objective is not to build another AI product.

The objective is to define the reference category for
Privacy-Governed AI Access.
