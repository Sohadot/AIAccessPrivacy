# AI Access Privacy Category Development Model

**Status:** Foundational Draft — Proposed for Ratification  
**Version:** 0.2.0  
**Canonical Repository:** `Sohadot/AIAccessPrivacy`  
**Primary Asset:** `aiaccess.privacy`  
**Asset Posture:** Permanent Sovereign Operating Asset  
**Development Model:** Capability-driven, evidence-governed, reference-first

---

## 1. Purpose

This document governs the continuous development of AI Access Privacy from an emerging category into a trusted, operational, machine-readable, and commercially sustainable reference infrastructure.

It is not a conventional product roadmap.

A conventional roadmap lists features to be built.

This model defines:

- which capabilities the category must acquire;
- which evidence is required before each capability is claimed;
- which governance gates must be passed before development advances;
- how public reference authority is separated from commercial services;
- how every layer remains transparent, reviewable, and continuously maintained;
- how the project may produce recurring revenue without compromising its independence.

The project shall not advance by accumulating pages, tools, or features.

It shall advance only when a new layer adds a durable capability to the category.

---

## 2. Governing Objective

AI Access Privacy exists to make the access relationships of artificial intelligence systems:

- explicit;
- bounded;
- machine-readable;
- evidence-linked;
- independently reviewable;
- continuously observable;
- revocable;
- operationally governable.

The long-term objective is to establish AI Access Privacy as the canonical reference category for governing how artificial intelligence systems access data, identities, tools, services, and execution capabilities.

The project is not being developed for resale.

Its value shall be maximized through:

- reference authority;
- conceptual precision;
- operational usefulness;
- accumulated evidence;
- machine-readable infrastructure;
- historical continuity;
- trusted adoption;
- recurring revenue;
- institutional durability.

## 3. Permanent Development Principles

### 3.1 Category before product

No product shall define the category retroactively.

The category must first establish its object, boundaries, language, principles, and operational implications.

Products shall be derived from the category.

---

### 3.2 Ontology before standardization

No formal standard shall be created for an object that has not been sufficiently understood.

Before standardization, the project must define:

- the relevant entities;
- the relationships between them;
- their states;
- their lifecycle;
- their evidence requirements;
- their governance boundaries.

---

### 3.3 Theory must be operationalizable

Conceptual strength alone is insufficient.

Every foundational concept must be capable of producing at least one of the following:

- a structured declaration;
- a machine-readable field;
- a repeatable assessment;
- a verifiable evidence requirement;
- a governance decision;
- a monitoring condition;
- a product capability.

A concept that cannot be operationalized remains a research hypothesis and must not be presented as a mature category primitive.

---

### 3.4 Evidence before claim

No claim may exceed the evidence supporting it.

The project shall distinguish permanently between:

- **Declared** — stated by the operator or subject;
- **Observed** — detected through inspection or automated analysis;
- **Verified** — supported by reviewable evidence;
- **Enforced** — technically imposed during operation;
- **Unknown** — not established by available evidence.

Payment, sponsorship, partnership, or commercial status shall never alter this distinction.

---

### 3.5 Transparency before authority

Reference authority shall not be asserted merely because the project publishes a standard.

Authority must emerge from:

- clear definitions;
- published methodology;
- source traceability;
- visible limitations;
- version history;
- correction procedures;
- consistent application;
- evidence-linked conclusions.

---

### 3.6 Capability-driven evolution

Each layer must add a distinct and durable capability.

The project shall not advance merely because a document was completed, a page was published, or a feature was shipped.

Advancement occurs only when the category becomes materially more capable of being understood, applied, verified, operated, adopted, or sustained.

---

### 3.7 Reference and commerce must remain separable

The public reference layer shall remain independent from paid services.

Commercial revenue may arise from:

- continuous monitoring;
- private registries;
- historical intelligence;
- integrations;
- enterprise controls;
- high-volume API access;
- managed verification workflows;
- operational reporting.

Commercial revenue shall not arise from:

- purchasing a favorable conclusion;
- suppressing negative evidence;
- paying for a higher trust classification;
- removing valid findings;
- undisclosed sponsored rankings;
- selling unsupported certification claims.

---

### 3.8 Continuous maintenance is part of the product

AI access is not static.

Permissions, integrations, tools, models, policies, endpoints, subprocessors, retention practices, and execution capabilities may change.

Therefore, the category shall not treat verification as a one-time event.

Continuous observation, change detection, version history, and expiry are first-class requirements.

---

## 4. Category Object

The primary governance object of AI Access Privacy is:

> **The operational access relationship between an artificial intelligence system and a protected resource or capability.**

The project does not govern the model in isolation.

It governs the conditions under which an AI system may interact with:

- data;
- identities;
- tools;
- APIs;
- services;
- applications;
- communication channels;
- financial functions;
- physical systems;
- execution capabilities.

An access relationship may include:

- a requesting actor;
- an operator;
- a protected resource;
- a declared purpose;
- an authorization context;
- permitted actions;
- prohibited actions;
- temporal boundaries;
- retention conditions;
- data-handling conditions;
- evidence;
- monitoring state;
- revocation state.

This definition remains provisional until ratified through the Access Ontology.

# 5. Capability Layer Model

The project shall evolve through seven capability layers.

Layers are cumulative.

A later layer may not invalidate the conceptual integrity of an earlier layer without a documented governance decision and versioned migration path.

---

## Layer 0 — Category Foundation

### Capability added

The category can be described, distinguished, and defended as an independent field.

### Required artifacts

- `CATEGORY_THESIS.md`
- `ACCESS_ONTOLOGY.md`
- `CATEGORY_BOUNDARY.md`
- `FIRST_PRINCIPLES.md`
- `CANONICAL_LANGUAGE.md`
- `CLAIMS_AND_EVIDENCE_POLICY.md`
- `DECISION_LOG.md`

### Required outcomes

The project must be able to explain:

- why the category exists;
- what its primary governance object is;
- how it differs from adjacent disciplines;
- what falls inside and outside its scope;
- which principles cannot be violated;
- which terms have canonical meanings;
- how claims relate to evidence.

### Completion gate

Layer 0 is complete only when:

- the category can be defined without reference to a specific vendor or product;
- its boundary against IAM, authorization, privacy regulation, AI safety, and cybersecurity is explicit;
- the access relationship is ontologically coherent;
- the category naturally implies operational artifacts;
- unresolved foundational disputes are recorded rather than hidden.

### Commercial state

No commercial claims are required.

The primary value created is conceptual defensibility and future product coherence.

## Layer 1 — Reference Structure

### Capability added

The category can be represented consistently and applied across different systems.

### Required artifacts

- AI Access Privacy Standard
- canonical vocabulary
- entity and relationship model
- lifecycle model
- conformance requirements
- machine-readable JSON Schema
- normative and non-normative examples
- versioning policy
- compatibility policy

### Required outcomes

The reference layer must define:

- what must be declared;
- what may be optional;
- how unknown values are represented;
- how evidence is attached;
- how versions are identified;
- how access changes are expressed;
- how revocation is represented;
- how conformance is determined.

### Completion gate

Layer 1 is complete only when:

- two independent implementers can produce structurally comparable declarations;
- the schema validates consistently;
- normative requirements are distinguishable from guidance;
- the model does not depend on one protocol, vendor, or ecosystem;
- backward compatibility rules are documented.

### Commercial state

The reference structure remains public.

Commercial use may begin through implementation assistance, but the standard itself shall not be paywalled.

---

## Layer 2 — Declaration and Evidence

### Capability added

AI access posture can be declared in a structured, machine-readable, and evidence-linked form.

### Core operational artifact

> **AI Access Passport**

### Required capabilities

- declaration generation;
- evidence attachment;
- source attribution;
- operator identification;
- validity period;
- version history;
- declaration status;
- verification status;
- revocation reference;
- cryptographic or signed integrity where appropriate.

### Required distinctions

Every Passport must distinguish between:

- self-declared information;
- automatically observed information;
- externally verified information;
- enforced controls;
- unavailable information.

### Completion gate

Layer 2 is complete only when:

- a Passport can be generated from the reference schema;
- each material claim has a status and provenance;
- missing information is represented explicitly;
- expired or revoked Passports cannot appear current;
- consumers can interpret the artifact without relying on a visual interface.

### Commercial state

Basic Passport creation remains free or openly accessible.

Paid services may include:

- managed declaration workflows;
- evidence hosting;
- signed declarations;
- operator verification;
- extended history;
- organizational management.

Payment shall not imply a favorable posture.

---

## Layer 3 — Registry and Verification

### Capability added

Declarations can be discovered, compared, reviewed, and verified through a maintained reference registry.

### Required capabilities

- public registry entries;
- canonical identifiers;
- Passport lookup;
- evidence references;
- verification levels;
- historical versions;
- correction submissions;
- dispute records;
- revocation status;
- machine-readable registry access.

### Verification model

Verification must be bounded.

The registry shall state:

- what was checked;
- when it was checked;
- by which method;
- against which version;
- using which evidence;
- what was not checked;
- when the result expires.

### Completion gate

Layer 3 is complete only when:

- registry entries are reproducible from documented sources;
- changes are append-only or historically recoverable;
- verification does not collapse into a single unsupported score;
- corrections and disputes have a documented process;
- commercial relationships are disclosed.

### Commercial state

Recurring revenue may begin through:

- verified operator services;
- extended history;
- evidence management;
- private registry spaces;
- bulk exports;
- higher API limits.

---

## Layer 4 — Continuous Observation

### Capability added

Changes in AI access posture can be detected, classified, and communicated over time.

### Core product capability

> **Continuous AI Access Permission Monitoring**

### Required capabilities

- baseline creation;
- scheduled reassessment;
- version comparison;
- permission change detection;
- tool and endpoint change detection;
- subprocessor and integration change detection;
- model or provider change detection;
- retention and data-handling change detection;
- revocation and expiry tracking;
- change classification (material vs. non-material);
- notification and alerting;
- historical change timeline.

### Required outcomes

The observation layer must be able to:

- establish a reference baseline for an access relationship;
- detect when a declared or observed posture changes;
- distinguish material changes from cosmetic ones;
- preserve a reviewable history of every change;
- express when an assessment has expired;
- communicate changes without requiring manual re-inspection.

### Completion gate

Layer 4 is complete only when:

- a baseline can be reproduced from documented sources;
- changes are detected without destroying prior state;
- material and non-material changes are classified consistently;
- expiry is enforced rather than assumed;
- notifications reflect evidence rather than speculation.

### Commercial state

Continuous observation is inherently a recurring-value capability.

Recurring revenue may arise from:

- scheduled monitoring;
- change alerting;
- historical timelines;
- drift and posture-change reporting;
- reassessment bound to a service level.

Free access may remain limited to point-in-time inspection.

---

## Layer 5 — Operational Governance

### Capability added

An organization can operate the category internally — governing, approving, and enforcing AI access posture across its own systems and the third parties it depends on.

This capability is not tied to a single customer type. It enables organized operation of AI access rather than the resale of a product to enterprises.

### Core product capability

> **AI Access Governance Operations**

### Required capabilities

- access inventories;
- private registries;
- policy rules;
- approval workflows;
- exception handling;
- enforcement workflows;
- role-based accountability;
- audit history;
- integrations with existing operational systems;
- organizational reporting.

### Required distinctions

Operational governance must distinguish permanently between:

- what is **declared**;
- what is **approved**;
- what is **enforced**;
- what is an **accepted exception**;
- what is **out of policy**.

These distinctions must not collapse into a single status.

### Boundary of enforcement

AIAccessPrivacy does not itself execute authorization. Actual enforcement — permitting, denying, or terminating an AI system's access at runtime — is the responsibility of external authorization, IAM, and policy systems.

Within this layer, AIAccessPrivacy defines and maintains only:

- governance state (declared, approved, enforced, accepted exception, out of policy);
- governance decisions (approvals, exceptions, revocation intent);
- the evidence and audit history supporting those decisions;
- integration signals emitted to, and consumed from, the external systems that perform enforcement.

The word "enforced" in this document is therefore a governance status recorded on the basis of evidence from those external systems, not an action performed by AIAccessPrivacy.

### Completion gate

Layer 5 is complete only when:

- an organization can maintain an inventory of its AI access relationships;
- policies can be expressed and evaluated against declarations;
- exceptions are recorded rather than hidden;
- approvals and enforcement produce an auditable trail;
- governance state is separable from public reference state.

### Commercial state

Operational governance is the primary layer for durable organizational revenue.

Recurring revenue may arise from:

- private registries and inventories;
- policy and approval workflows;
- enforcement and exception management;
- audit and compliance reporting;
- integrations and high-volume API access.

The public reference layer shall remain independent from these paid controls. Purchasing operational tooling shall never alter a public posture, verification level, or evidence classification.

---

## Layer 6 — Ecosystem and Institutional Durability

### Capability added

The category can survive and spread beyond any single operator, interface, or product.

This is the highest currently defined capability layer. It does not mean the project is "finished." It means the category has become capable of persisting and being adopted independently of the founding operator.

### Required capabilities and conditions

- independent implementations;
- SDKs and interoperability;
- conformance testing;
- external adoption;
- ecosystem extensions;
- governance continuity;
- historical preservation;
- reduced dependence on the founding operator.

### Required outcomes

The durability layer must ensure that:

- the reference can be implemented by parties other than the founder;
- conformance can be verified independently;
- the category's history is preserved and recoverable;
- governance can continue across changes in stewardship;
- adoption does not depend on a single commercial interface.

### Maturity conditions

Layer 6 is not "completed" in the sense of being finished and closed.

It is measured by maturity conditions, including:

- at least one independent, conformant implementation exists;
- conformance can be verified by a third party;
- the reference survives the loss of any single operator;
- historical records remain accessible and tamper-evident;
- canonical reference authority is earned and continuously maintained rather than self-asserted.

### On canonical reference authority

Canonical or institutional reference authority is a **maturity condition within this layer, not a guaranteed endpoint and not a separate layer to be built and completed.**

It must be continuously earned through transparency, consistency, and independent corroboration. It can be lost if evidence, maintenance, or neutrality lapse. For this reason it is expressed here as a condition to be acquired and preserved, not as a feature whose delivery concludes the project.

### Commercial state

Durability protects long-term value rather than generating direct revenue.

Sustainable revenue from earlier layers funds the neutrality, maintenance, and continuity that durability requires.

## 6. Layer Transition and Downgrade Rules

### 6.1 Advancement

Advancement from one layer to the next requires that the earlier layer's completion gate (or, for Layer 6, its maturity conditions) be met and that the transition be recorded in `DECISION_LOG.md`.

A layer is not entered because a document was written; it is entered because the preceding capability is demonstrably present.

### 6.2 Exploratory prototypes

Exploratory prototypes of later-layer capabilities are permitted before those layers are formally entered, provided they are:

- **non-normative** — they do not define or constrain the reference, schema, or canonical language;
- **isolated** — they are kept separate from public reference material and from any layer's delivered artifacts (for example, on an exploratory branch such as `EXP-001`);
- clearly labeled as experimental.

Such prototypes shall never be counted as layer entry, as maturity, or as conformance, and shall not be cited as evidence that a completion gate or maturity condition has been met. Their existence changes no layer's status.

### 6.3 Downgrade and regression

Layer status is not permanent. A capability that was previously demonstrated may be lost.

A layer or a specific claim shall be downgraded when:

- the evidence supporting it expires or is withdrawn;
- continuous observation is no longer maintained;
- a maturity condition ceases to hold;
- an independent implementation or verification is no longer available;
- a governance obligation is not met.

A downgrade must be recorded in `DECISION_LOG.md` with its cause. Regression shall be represented honestly rather than concealed, in the same way that missing evidence is represented explicitly rather than assumed favorable.

---

## 7. Separation of Reference Authority and Commerce

The separation established in Principle 3.7 applies across every layer and is restated here as a permanent structural constraint:

- The public reference — definitions, schema, methodology, and canonical language — shall remain independent of paid services.
- Commercial revenue may fund neutrality and continuity but shall never purchase a conclusion, verification level, ranking, or evidence classification.
- Operational governance tooling (Layer 5) and durability infrastructure (Layer 6) shall not create a private path to alter public posture.

Where a commercial relationship exists, it shall be disclosed.

---

## 8. Long-Term Value Accumulation

The project's value compounds through accumulation rather than through releases.

Value accumulates in:

- reference authority earned through transparency;
- accumulated, evidence-linked history;
- machine-readable infrastructure that others can build on;
- trusted adoption independent of a single interface;
- recurring revenue that sustains independence;
- institutional continuity across changes in stewardship.

No single release completes this accumulation. Each layer adds a durable capability that the following layers depend upon.

## 10. Amendment and Ratification

This document is a **Foundational Draft — Proposed for Ratification**.

- It is proposed via a review branch and a draft pull request into `main`.
- It shall not be merged before a full line-by-line review of its layer count, transition conditions, separation of reference authority from commerce, and consistency with the repository's actual state.
- **Merge and ratification are distinct events.** Merging this document into `main` does not ratify it. On merge its status remains **Foundational Draft — Proposed for Ratification**; merge only makes the proposed draft the reviewed working text on `main`.
- The status shall become **Ratified Foundational Governance Artifact** only when both conditions are met: `DECISION_LOG.md` exists, and an independent decision by the repository owner recording ratification is entered in it. Until that recorded decision exists, no merge, tag, or release confers ratified status.
- A status change that does not alter normative content does not require a version increment; it is sufficient to record the ratification decision in `DECISION_LOG.md`.

Substantive amendments to the normative content of any layer shall increment the version and be recorded in `DECISION_LOG.md`.
