# AI Access Privacy Category Development Model

**Status:** Foundational Governance Artifact  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Canonical Repository:** `Sohadot/AIAccessPrivacy`  
**Primary Asset:** `aiaccess.privacy`  
**Asset Posture:** Permanent Sovereign Operating Asset  
**Development Model:** Capability-driven, evidence-governed, reference-first

---

## 1. Purpose

This document governs the continuous development of AI Access Privacy from an emerging category into a trusted, operational, machine-readable, and commercially sustainable reference infrastructure.

It is not a conventional product roadmap.

A conventional roadmap lists features to be built.

This model defines:

- which capabilities the category must acquire;
- which evidence is required before each capability is claimed;
- which governance gates must be passed before development advances;
- how public reference authority is separated from commercial services;
- how every layer remains transparent, reviewable, and continuously maintained;
- how the project may produce recurring revenue without compromising its independence.

The project shall not advance by accumulating pages, tools, or features.

It shall advance only when a new layer adds a durable capability to the category.

---

## 2. Governing Objective

AI Access Privacy exists to make the access relationships of artificial intelligence systems:

- explicit;
- bounded;
- machine-readable;
- evidence-linked;
- independently reviewable;
- continuously observable;
- revocable;
- operationally governable.

The long-term objective is to establish AI Access Privacy as the canonical reference category for governing how artificial intelligence systems access data, identities, tools, services, and execution capabilities.

The project is not being developed for resale.

Its value shall be maximized through:

- reference authority;
- conceptual precision;
- operational usefulness;
- accumulated evidence;
- machine-readable infrastructure;
- historical continuity;
- trusted adoption;
- recurring revenue;
- institutional durability.

---

## 3. Permanent Development Principles

### 3.1 Category before product

No product shall define the category retroactively.

The category must first establish its object, boundaries, language, principles, and operational implications.

Products shall be derived from the category.

---

### 3.2 Ontology before standardization

No formal standard shall be created for an object that has not been sufficiently understood.

Before standardization, the project must define:

- the relevant entities;
- the relationships between them;
- their states;
- their lifecycle;
- their evidence requirements;
- their governance boundaries.

---

### 3.3 Theory must be operationalizable

Conceptual strength alone is insufficient.

Every foundational concept must be capable of producing at least one of the following:

- a structured declaration;
- a machine-readable field;
- a repeatable assessment;
- a verifiable evidence requirement;
- a governance decision;
- a monitoring condition;
- a product capability.

A concept that cannot be operationalized remains a research hypothesis and must not be presented as a mature category primitive.

---

### 3.4 Evidence before claim

No claim may exceed the evidence supporting it.

The project shall distinguish permanently between:

- **Declared** — stated by the operator or subject;
- **Observed** — detected through inspection or automated analysis;
- **Verified** — supported by reviewable evidence;
- **Enforced** — technically imposed during operation;
- **Unknown** — not established by available evidence.

Payment, sponsorship, partnership, or commercial status shall never alter this distinction.

---

### 3.5 Transparency before authority

Reference authority shall not be asserted merely because the project publishes a standard.

Authority must emerge from:

- clear definitions;
- published methodology;
- source traceability;
- visible limitations;
- version history;
- correction procedures;
- consistent application;
- evidence-linked conclusions.

---

### 3.6 Capability-driven evolution

Each layer must add a distinct and durable capability.

The project shall not advance merely because a document was completed, a page was published, or a feature was shipped.

Advancement occurs only when the category becomes materially more capable of being understood, applied, verified, operated, adopted, or sustained.

---

### 3.7 Reference and commerce must remain separable

The public reference layer shall remain independent from paid services.

Commercial revenue may arise from:

- continuous monitoring;
- private registries;
- historical intelligence;
- integrations;
- enterprise controls;
- high-volume API access;
- managed verification workflows;
- operational reporting.

Commercial revenue shall not arise from:

- purchasing a favorable conclusion;
- suppressing negative evidence;
- paying for a higher trust classification;
- removing valid findings;
- undisclosed sponsored rankings;
- selling unsupported certification claims.

---

### 3.8 Continuous maintenance is part of the product

AI access is not static.

Permissions, integrations, tools, models, policies, endpoints, subprocessors, retention practices, and execution capabilities may change.

Therefore, the category shall not treat verification as a one-time event.

Continuous observation, change detection, version history, and expiry are first-class requirements.

---

## 4. Category Object

The primary governance object of AI Access Privacy is:

> **The operational access relationship between an artificial intelligence system and a protected resource or capability.**

The project does not govern the model in isolation.

It governs the conditions under which an AI system may interact with:

- data;
- identities;
- tools;
- APIs;
- services;
- applications;
- communication channels;
- financial functions;
- physical systems;
- execution capabilities.

An access relationship may include:

- a requesting actor;
- an operator;
- a protected resource;
- a declared purpose;
- an authorization context;
- permitted actions;
- prohibited actions;
- temporal boundaries;
- retention conditions;
- data-handling conditions;
- evidence;
- monitoring state;
- revocation state.

This definition remains provisional until ratified through the Access Ontology.

# 5. Capability Layer Model

The project shall evolve through seven capability layers.

Layers are cumulative.

A later layer may not invalidate the conceptual integrity of an earlier layer without a documented governance decision and versioned migration path.

---

## Layer 0 — Category Foundation

### Capability added

The category can be described, distinguished, and defended as an independent field.

### Required artifacts

- `CATEGORY_THESIS.md`
- `ACCESS_ONTOLOGY.md`
- `CATEGORY_BOUNDARY.md`
- `FIRST_PRINCIPLES.md`
- `CANONICAL_LANGUAGE.md`
- `CLAIMS_AND_EVIDENCE_POLICY.md`
- `DECISION_LOG.md`

### Required outcomes

The project must be able to explain:

- why the category exists;
- what its primary governance object is;
- how it differs from adjacent disciplines;
- what falls inside and outside its scope;
- which principles cannot be violated;
- which terms have canonical meanings;
- how claims relate to evidence.

### Completion gate

Layer 0 is complete only when:

- the category can be defined without reference to a specific vendor or product;
- its boundary against IAM, authorization, privacy regulation, AI safety, and cybersecurity is explicit;
- the access relationship is ontologically coherent;
- the category naturally implies operational artifacts;
- unresolved foundational disputes are recorded rather than hidden.

### Commercial state

No commercial claims are required.

The primary value created is conceptual defensibility and future product coherence.

---

## Layer 1 — Reference Structure

### Capability added

The category can be represented consistently and applied across different systems.

### Required artifacts

- AI Access Privacy Standard
- canonical vocabulary
- entity and relationship model
- lifecycle model
- conformance requirements
- machine-readable JSON Schema
- normative and non-normative examples
- versioning policy
- compatibility policy

### Required outcomes

The reference layer must define:

- what must be declared;
- what may be optional;
- how unknown values are represented;
- how evidence is attached;
- how versions are identified;
- how access changes are expressed;
- how revocation is represented;
- how conformance is determined.

### Completion gate

Layer 1 is complete only when:

- two independent implementers can produce structurally comparable declarations;
- the schema validates consistently;
- normative requirements are distinguishable from guidance;
- the model does not depend on one protocol, vendor, or ecosystem;
- backward compatibility rules are documented.

### Commercial state

The reference structure remains public.

Commercial use may begin through implementation assistance, but the standard itself shall not be paywalled.

---

## Layer 2 — Declaration and Evidence

### Capability added

AI access posture can be declared in a structured, machine-readable, and evidence-linked form.

### Core operational artifact

> **AI Access Passport**

### Required capabilities

- declaration generation;
- evidence attachment;
- source attribution;
- operator identification;
- validity period;
- version history;
- declaration status;
- verification status;
- revocation reference;
- cryptographic or signed integrity where appropriate.

### Required distinctions

Every Passport must distinguish between:

- self-declared information;
- automatically observed information;
- externally verified information;
- enforced controls;
- unavailable information.

### Completion gate

Layer 2 is complete only when:

- a Passport can be generated from the reference schema;
- each material claim has a status and provenance;
- missing information is represented explicitly;
- expired or revoked Passports cannot appear current;
- consumers can interpret the artifact without relying on a visual interface.

### Commercial state

Basic Passport creation remains free or openly accessible.

Paid services may include:

- managed declaration workflows;
- evidence hosting;
- signed declarations;
- operator verification;
- extended history;
- organizational management.

Payment shall not imply a favorable posture.

---

## Layer 3 — Registry and Verification

### Capability added

Declarations can be discovered, compared, reviewed, and verified through a maintained reference registry.

### Required capabilities

- public registry entries;
- canonical identifiers;
- Passport lookup;
- evidence references;
- verification levels;
- historical versions;
- correction submissions;
- dispute records;
- revocation status;
- machine-readable registry access.

### Verification model

Verification must be bounded.

The registry shall state:

- what was checked;
- when it was checked;
- by which method;
- against which version;
- using which evidence;
- what was not checked;
- when the result expires.

### Completion gate

Layer 3 is complete only when:

- registry entries are reproducible from documented sources;
- changes are append-only or historically recoverable;
- verification does not collapse into a single unsupported score;
- corrections and disputes have a documented process;
- commercial relationships are disclosed.

### Commercial state

Recurring revenue may begin through:

- verified operator services;
- extended history;
- evidence management;
- private registry spaces;
- bulk exports;
- higher API limits.

---

## Layer 4 — Continuous Observation

### Capability added

Changes in AI access posture can be detected, classified, and communicated over time.

### Core product capability

> **Continuous AI Access Permission Monitoring**

### Required capabilities

- baseline creation;
- scheduled reassessment;
- version comparison;
- permission change detection;
- tool and endpoint change detection
